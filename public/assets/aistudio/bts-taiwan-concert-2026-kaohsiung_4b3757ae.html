The ticket story: queues, prayers and national news

Kaohsiung's June on-sale became a story in itself. With demand far outstripping supply, Taiwanese ARMY went viral for visiting temples to pray to the God of Love for ticketing luck — WION, Taiwan News and Interaksyon all covered the queues, turning the on-sale into national news. That's the honest baseline: assume face-value tickets are gone.

What's actually left, in order of realism:

  1. Official production releases — final seat releases sometimes appear close to show dates through the official ticketing partner. Watch the promoter's official channels only.
  2. Authorised resale — if the Taiwanese promoter designated an official resale platform, that's the only resale worth touching.
  3. Cinema live-viewing — the ARIRANG tour broadcasts to roughly 6,100 cinemas worldwide, and Taiwan's cinema chains have participated in the tour's live-viewing dates through 2026. Big screen, full ARMY crowd, a fraction of the stress.

Getting there and around — practical specifics

Kaohsiung is one of the easiest stadium-cities in Asia to navigate:

  • Flying in: Kaohsiung International Airport (KHH) serves regional routes directly — often cheaper and faster than flying into Taipei for a Kaohsiung event.
  • From Taipei: the THSR high-speed rail runs Taipei → Zuoying in roughly 90–120 minutes; Zuoying station connects onward to the city's metro (KRTC) and the stadium district. This is the standard fan route.
  • Show nights: expect packed KRTC trains and gridlocked roads near the venue for hours. Travel early, eat early, and buy a metro card on day one — it works on buses too.
  • Three-show warning: three consecutive stadium-scale crowds mean queues everywhere — build buffer time into everything from hotel check-in to dinner.

Hotels: the January cautionary tale

One thing to know before booking: Kaohsiung hotels were accused of overcharging around the concert dates back in January (Taiwan News), and the city investigated price gouging (Taipei Times, January 18). Local authorities took the complaints seriously — but the smart-play rules still apply:

  • Compare rates across platforms before booking — screenshot the differences
  • Book refundable where possible until the official dates/doors are confirmed from the promoter
  • Ignore unofficial "ARIRANG packages" — the only packages worth buying come from the city's own announced offers

If you're going ticketless: the Kaohsiung game plan

Honestly? Kaohsiung might be the best city on the tour to be ticketless:

  1. Book the trip — flights into KHH and a central hotel near the metro
  2. Hit the city's announced food promotions and concertgoer offers
  3. Soak up show-day atmosphere around the venue district (fans, photo ops, merch pop-ups if present)
  4. Book a cinema live-viewing seat at a local chain for the full-production experience
  5. Stay for the city itself — Kaohsiung's harbor, night markets and riverside make it a proper trip, not just a concert run
