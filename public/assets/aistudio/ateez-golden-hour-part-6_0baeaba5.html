How the comeback was rolled out — day by day

KQ Entertainment announced the album on October 7–8 with a cryptic "CRASH TO BEGIN" spoiler video — and confirmed the line fans had been bracing for: Part.6 ends the GOLDEN HOUR series. The rollout since has been a masterclass in pacing:

  • October 23: pre-release single "Drip" dropped — and landed on the Billboard Global 200 in its first chart week, with Korean press describing the group's chart rise as "scary" (their word, not ours).
  • October 25: the 5-track tracklist was revealed, confirming "UP" as title track, plus the first MV teaser.
  • November 5: a full album preview landed on YouTube — the "Part.6 Preview" — alongside news of one-night-only release parties.
  • November 10: a new "UP" MV teaser dropped, teasing an expanded-universe concept for the video.
  • November 13: album + "UP" MV at release. The series closes.

"Drip" — the statement the album walks in on

Pre-release singles set the temperature, and "Drip" set it high: a groovy, sleek MV, immediate fan traction, and a Billboard Global 200 debut in week one — before the album even had a tracklist. The Korea Herald flagged ATEEZ's chart milestone with "Drip" in early November, and the Sports Chosun "rise getting scary" framing captured the consensus: ATEEZ's global numbers have stopped being a surprise and started being a pattern.

What "the end of GOLDEN HOUR" means

Series finales in K-pop are rare on this scale — most multi-part runs fizzle or pivot. ATEEZ closing GOLDEN HOUR after three Billboard No. 1s, a Billboard Global 200 pre-release, and a global tour cycle means the group is ending its hottest streak on a deliberate high note. Whatever comes after Part.6 starts from a taller summit than the series did.
